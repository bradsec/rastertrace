    Tracing settings

    Controls for turning raster artwork into clean vector paths.

    Color presets & Max colors
    Maximum colors in the output. Fewer colors means simpler, cleaner, smaller SVGs. A preset is a quick way to pick a color count and changes nothing else.
    Color source
    This image picks the colors automatically. My colors snaps every shape to a palette you supply: paste hex codes or import an Adobe .ase, .gpl, or hex list. Max colors is disabled while My colors has swatches.
    Smallest shape
    Drops traced specks smaller than this many pixels. Raise it to remove noise, dust, and JPEG artifacts.
    Merge similar shades
    Minimum color difference between stacked layers. Raise it to merge similar shades into one shape.
    Upscale before tracing
    Enlarges the image before tracing for smoother curves. Auto fits 2048 px; Ultra fits 4096 px for fabrication (slower).
    Path mode
    Spline traces smooth curves (most artwork), Polygon straight segments (hard-edged geometry), Pixel exact outlines (pixel art).
    Simplify
    How closely paths follow the image, like Illustrator's Paths slider in reverse. Lower keeps detail; higher gives fewer, smoother points. Shows Custom when the Advanced tracing curve settings were set by hand. Spline mode only.
    Corner rounding
    Angles smoother than this trace as curves. Lower keeps text and logo corners sharp; higher flows. Spline mode only.
    Layering
    Stacked layers shapes on top of each other (smallest files). Cutout makes non-overlapping shapes that tile edge to edge, for cutting machines.
    Advanced tracing
    Path precision: decimal places in coordinates (1-2 small web files, 3-4 fabrication). Segment length and spline splitting trade curve fidelity for simplicity; they apply to Spline mode only.
    Stencil, threshold & ink
    Black & white trace: pixels darker than the threshold become shapes, everything else drops out. Ink chooses black shapes on a transparent background, or white shapes on a black backing. Best for laser cutting, stamps, silhouettes. Max colors, Merge similar shades and Grayscale do not apply while it is on.
    Grayscale & Denoise
    Grayscale converts to gray tones before tracing. Denoise smooths photo noise (softens pixel art).
    Background removal
    Makes the background transparent: connected-from-edges, auto-detected color, or a picked color. A picked color can be limited to areas connected to the edges, and edge removal can also clear enclosed background or follow a gradient background. Tolerance widens the match; clean specks removes stray dots and pinholes; edge smooth rounds off jagged cutout edges (corners round and details thinner than the setting disappear); edge trim and defringe clean the cutout boundary.
    Palette
    Changes a traced color to a new HEX or RGB value without retracing; shapes stay the same. Use Pick from result and click a color in the result, or click a swatch when the trace has 32 colors or fewer. To merge similar colors, Shift- or Ctrl-click swatches (or press Select and tap them), then Merge: they all take the color of the largest one and show as one swatch with a count. Colors you set to the same value merge too. Split colors on a merged swatch restores each one. Edits apply to the preview and every export, are kept when a retrace still has that color, and clear when you open a new image. With Colors set to All, edits match exact traced colors only. Hide color makes a color transparent, exact in Cutout layering. In Stacked layering, hiding a color reveals the shape beneath it; hiding the background color makes it transparent. Click Use cutout layering in the palette panel to switch with one click.

    Vector cleanup

    Remove unwanted areas from the traced vector result. The tools sit on the left edge of the preview, and the chosen tool's options show in the strip above it. On phones the rail shows the chosen tool; the grid button opens every tool, and its options open in a panel at the bottom of the screen.

    Eraser
    Press E or select the eraser icon, then drag over unwanted vector areas, including from outside an image edge. Use [ and ] to decrease or increase its diameter. The circular cursor shows the affected size. On a touchscreen, put a second finger down to pinch-zoom instead; the stroke in progress is undone.
    Blob fill
    Press B or select the brush icon, choose or enter a fill color, then paint over the vector result. Use the eyedropper to sample from the active traced palette after color reduction and preprocessing. The eraser and blob brush share the Size control.
    Box & circle marquee
    Press M for a rectangular selection or Shift+M for an elliptical selection. Drag anywhere in the preview, including outside the image. Press Shift during the drag for a square or circle, or Alt/Option to draw from the center.
    Add to or subtract from a selection
    Hold Shift when you start a marquee, lasso or wand click to add it to the current selection, or Alt/Option to subtract it; the cursor shows a + or −. On touch screens, use the New, Add and Subtract buttons in the tool options. Switching selection tools keeps the selection, so a wand area can be combined with a marquee.
    Polygonal lasso
    Press L, then click point-to-point around an irregular area. Hold Shift to lock a segment to 45-degree increments. Double-click, press Enter, or select Close shape to close it. While drawing, Backspace or Esc removes the last point.
    Magic wand
    Press W or select the wand icon, then click a color in the result to select that connected area only; the same color elsewhere stays unselected. Shift+click adds more areas. Tolerance also takes similar shades, Expand grows the selection to cover leftover edges, and Smooth rounds off jagged pixel steps; each updates the current selection. Erase makes the area transparent, or Fill paints it with the brush color.
    Erase or fill a selection
    After closing a selection, select Erase or Fill below the preview, or press Delete or Backspace to erase it and Ctrl/Cmd+Backspace to fill it with the current blob-brush color. Cancel drops the selection. Areas outside the image are ignored, so cleanup affects only the vector result.
    Undo, redo & restore
    Use Ctrl/Cmd+Z to undo and Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl/Cmd+Y to redo. Restore Original removes every manual cleanup action.
    Exporting cleanup
    Manual cleanup is preserved in SVG and PNG exports. PDF and DXF are unavailable while cleanup masks are present.

    SVG export guide

    Choose an output profile, size, and format for your destination.

    Profiles

    Export profiles are purpose-based starting points: they set colors, cleanup, path detail, and output options together. Every control stays editable afterwards; changing one switches the profile back to Custom.

    Size & units

    SVG is vector geometry, so there is no DPI: it scales cleanly at any size. Use Match source pixels for websites and apps. Use Custom size to set a width in px, mm, cm, or in; the height follows the image automatically. Physical units let print and cutting software open the file at its real-world size.

    Smallest files

    The Web profile combines fewer colors, path precision 1, and minified output. Same-fill shapes are always grouped to save bytes. Fewer colors and higher speckle filtering shrink files far more than any other setting.

    Shape fidelity

    Max detail traces smooth curves that hug the source edges closely, with minimal despeckle. Pixel exact skips curve fitting entirely: the boundary of every quantized color region is traced literally, pixel for pixel.

    Laser & cutting

    For laser work use the Laser stencil profile: black & white trace, aggressive despeckle, physical size. Set upscale to Ultra (fit 4096) for the cleanest edges, and try Cutout layering for vinyl and CNC. Photos usually engrave better exported as a prepared raster image than as a traced SVG. For general-purpose crisp silhouettes (stickers, decals, icon marks) use Mono stencil (black) or Mono stencil (white) instead.
